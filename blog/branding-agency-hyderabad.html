How to choose a branding agency in Hyderabad

By Pluto Theory · 13 November 2026 · 8 min read

Short answer: pick the branding agency in Hyderabad that asks you the most useful questions before it quotes, shows a clear process with a strategy step, names the people who will actually do your work, and puts the deliverables and copyright transfer in writing. As of November 2026, published price lists put a complete brand identity at roughly ₹1.5 lakh to ₹5 lakh at established studios, with budget packages from about ₹15,000 and strategy-led projects reaching ₹15 lakh or more. Compare quotes line by line on scope, never on the total alone.

This guide is for founders and marketing teams hiring for the first time, or hiring again after a project that went wrong. It covers what you should get, what it costs, what to ask, and the warning signs that are easy to miss in a friendly first call.

What a branding agency should actually deliver

"Branding" means very different things on different quotes. Before you compare prices, know which of these layers you are buying:

LayerWhat it includesDo you need it now?
Discovery and strategyInterviews, a look at competitors and customers, positioning, what the brand stands forYes, even in a short form. Design without it is guesswork.
NamingName options, basic trademark, domain and handle checksOnly if you are new or renaming
Visual identityLogo and its variants, colours with exact codes, typefaces, layout style, imagery directionYes
Verbal identityTone of voice, tagline, key messages, example linesYes, at least the basics
Brand guidelinesOne document with the visual and verbal rules, so your team and vendors stay consistentYes, short is fine
ApplicationsBusiness cards, social templates, packaging, signage, pitch deck, website designOnly the ones you will use in the next six months

Questions to ask before you sign

  1. What do you need to know from us before you recommend anything? A good agency has questions. One that already has a package for you before hearing your business is selling scope.
  2. What happens in discovery, and how long does it take? Ask what they will read, who they will speak to, and what you will see at the end of it.
  3. Who will work on our project day to day? Ask for names and roles. The person who pitched may not be the person who designs.
  4. Can we see work close to our category or stage? Ask them to walk you through the reasoning behind one project, not just the final images.
  5. How many concepts and revision rounds are included? And what happens, and what it costs, if you need more.
  6. Exactly what files and documents will we receive? Get the list in writing: logo formats, colour codes, fonts, guidelines, templates.
  7. Will you assign us the copyright in writing? See the ownership section below. Also ask who pays for font licences.
  8. What is the payment schedule? Part up front and the rest on milestones is normal. Paying everything before work starts is not.
  9. Tell us about a project that did not go to plan. How they answer tells you how they will handle a disagreement with you.
  10. Can we speak to a past client? Ask that client how the agency handled feedback and deadlines.

Red flags

  • A proposal before a real conversation. If they quote from a one-line enquiry, the work will be generic too.
  • No price, no range. You do not need a fixed number on day one, but a serious studio can give you a range once it understands the brief.
  • Every logo in the portfolio looks alike. That usually means the studio has one style and will apply it to you whatever your business needs.
  • They cannot explain why a design works. "It looks clean" is not a reason. Ask how a choice connects to your customers or your positioning.
  • They never push back. If they agree with everything you say in the first meeting, they will not tell you when an idea is weak.
  • Vague deliverables. "Complete branding" with no itemised list leads to arguments later about what was promised.
  • A long retainer before any work. A branding project has a beginning and an end. Ongoing support can come after.
  • Guaranteed results in weeks. A new identity can change how people see you fast, but sales and recognition take longer and depend on far more than the logo.
  • Hesitation about ownership. If they will not transfer copyright in the final files, walk away.

Who owns the logo: get it in writing

Many founders assume that paying for a logo means owning it. Under India's Copyright Act, 1957, that is not automatic. Section 19 says an assignment of copyright is valid only if it is in writing and signed. It should name the work, the rights, the duration and the territory. If the duration is not stated, the assignment is treated as five years. If the territory is not stated, it is treated as India only. We read the text of the Act in November 2026.

For a logo you plan to use for decades, and maybe abroad, that matters. Ask the agency for a written assignment that covers all final logos and brand assets, worldwide, with no time limit. Ask separately about fonts: typefaces are licensed, not owned, and the licence should be in your company's name. This is general information, not legal advice. For anything you will build a company on, have a lawyer read the contract, and register your trademark through the IP India process.

How to compare quotes fairly

Send every agency the same short brief: what you do, who your customers are, three competitors, brands you admire and brands you do not want to resemble, what you need in the next six months, your deadline and a budget range. Then put the quotes side by side and check each line:

CheckWhat to look for
StrategyIs there a discovery or strategy phase, and what do you get from it?
ConceptsHow many logo directions, and how many revision rounds?
SystemColours, type, imagery style and voice, or only a logo?
GuidelinesA real document, or a one-page sheet of colour codes?
ApplicationsWhich templates, packaging or deck pages are included?
Files and rightsAll formats, editable source files, written copyright assignment
PriceIs GST included? What triggers extra charges?

A cheaper quote that leaves out strategy and guidelines can cost more later, when you pay someone to fix an identity your team cannot apply consistently.

Agency, studio or freelancer?

A skilled freelancer is often the right choice for a tight budget and a small scope, such as a logo and colours for a new venture. A studio or agency makes more sense when the project needs strategy, naming, a guide and many applications at once, because several people test the thinking. Size alone tells you little. Judge each option on process, past work and how well they listen.

Also think about what comes after the logo. Your brand will mostly be seen through photos on your website, marketplace listings and social feeds. If the studio that builds your identity can also photograph your products in Hyderabad, or create AI photoshoots in the same style, the brand stays consistent from the guide to the feed. Questions people ask

How much does a branding agency cost in Hyderabad?

Price lists published by Hyderabad agencies in November 2026 put a logo-only job at roughly ₹5,000 to ₹1 lakh, a complete brand identity at roughly ₹1.5 lakh to ₹5 lakh at established studios, and strategy plus identity at ₹5 lakh to ₹15 lakh or more. Budget starter packages start lower, around ₹15,000 to ₹35,000, with much less strategy work.

How long does a branding project take?

Published timelines from agencies in India range from about two to four weeks for a refresh, three to ten weeks for a full identity, and three to five months when research and strategy are included. Slow feedback rounds can stretch any of these.

Do I own the logo once I pay the agency?

Not automatically. Under Section 19 of India's Copyright Act, an assignment of copyright must be in writing and signed, and if it does not state a duration or territory it is treated as five years and India only. Ask for a written assignment that covers all final files, worldwide and with no time limit.

Should I hire a freelancer or a branding agency?

A good freelancer suits a tight budget and a small scope, such as a logo and colours. A studio or agency suits work that needs strategy, naming, a guide and many applications, because more people check the thinking. Judge either on their process and past work, not on their size.
