How to automate lead follow-up with AI (website, ads and WhatsApp)

By Pluto Theory · 20 November 2026 · 8 min read

Short answer: to automate lead follow-up, send every enquiry from your website form, ad lead forms and WhatsApp into one place, send an instant acknowledgement, record the lead with an owner and a stage, and schedule the next follow-ups from a short sequence you wrote. AI reads each enquiry, tags it and drafts the replies; a person approves anything that is not routine. Start with one lead source and one sequence, and measure how many leads get a reply and how quickly before you add more.

This guide is for founders and small sales teams who get enquiries from several places and lose some of them between the inbox, a WhatsApp chat and a spreadsheet. It covers what to automate, the order to build it in, the rules that apply in India and what to keep human. Examples are hypothetical and show how the work is set up, not results from a client.

Why leads slip through follow-up

Most lost leads are not rejections. They are enquiries that nobody answered on the day, a promised call that did not happen, or a quote that went out and was never chased. The causes are usually ordinary:

  • Enquiries arrive in different places. A website form lands in email, an ad lead form sits in the ad platform, and an Instagram or WhatsApp message sits on one person's phone.
  • Nobody owns the next step. A lead is "with the team", which means with nobody.
  • Follow-up depends on memory. The salesperson who is busy with today's customers forgets yesterday's quote.
  • Replies are written from scratch every time, so they are slow when the team is busy.

Speed matters, though the evidence deserves care. Several lead-response studies find that contacting someone soon after they enquire improves your chances. Most of them come from software vendors and measure different things in different industries, so we do not quote a single multiplier here. The practical reading is simple: acknowledge fast, and make sure a person follows up on a stated schedule.

The five parts of an automated follow-up process

  1. Capture. Every source writes to one list or CRM: the website form, ad lead forms, WhatsApp click-to-chat, Instagram messages and calls someone logs by hand. Record the source, the time and exactly what the person asked for.
  2. Acknowledge. Within a minute or two the person gets a reply that confirms the enquiry, says what happens next and by when. This one can be fully automatic because it makes no promise a person cannot keep.
  3. Qualify and route. AI reads the enquiry and tags it with product, budget band, location and urgency using rules you set, then assigns an owner. For example, a dental clinic could send implant enquiries to the senior dentist's coordinator and cleaning enquiries to the front desk.
  4. Follow up on a schedule. A short sequence runs until the lead replies, books or is marked closed: a first reply drafted from your own wording, a reminder after a couple of days, a last note a week later. Drafts go to a person to approve, at least at first.
  5. Report. Each week you can see how many leads came in, how many got a reply, how long the first reply took, which source they came from and how many are stuck at each stage.

Following up on WhatsApp

For most Indian businesses, WhatsApp is where leads actually reply. Meta's rules for the WhatsApp Business Platform shape what you can automate:

  • When a person messages you, a 24-hour customer service window opens in which you can reply with free-form text.
  • Outside that window you can only send pre-approved template messages, and templates are classed as marketing, utility or authentication. Meta charges for template messages by category, and its rates and free allowances have changed more than once, including in India. Check the current price list in Meta's developer documentation before you budget.
  • Marketing messages need the person's opt-in. A lead who messaged you first has opened a conversation, but that is not blanket permission to send promotions later.
  • Ordinary WhatsApp on a personal number is not meant for automated bulk sending, and numbers that do it risk being restricted.

A sensible pattern is: the person messages or fills a form that includes a WhatsApp opt-in, an automated message acknowledges them, the AI drafts replies to their questions from your own price list and FAQ, and a person takes over as soon as the conversation turns into a quote or a booking.

Rules to respect in India

This is a practical summary, not legal advice. Check your own case with a lawyer.

  • Commercial SMS and calls. The Telecom Commercial Communications Customer Preference Regulations (TCCCPR), notified by TRAI in July 2018, require businesses sending commercial SMS and voice messages to register their sender headers and message templates on the DLT platform run by telecom operators, and promotional messages are filtered against customers' Do Not Disturb preferences. Your SMS provider will usually walk you through registration.
  • Consent and records. Keep a record of when and how each person agreed to hear from you, and make opting out simple. Vendor guides to the rules stress that a general terms-and-conditions tick-box is a weak form of consent.
  • Personal data. Leads' names, numbers and messages are personal data under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. Collect what you need, say what you will use it for, and write down which tools and AI services see it. Use business accounts with AI providers.

Where AI helps, and where a person stays

Plain automation handles the moving and timing: copying leads into one place, sending the acknowledgement, scheduling reminders and stopping them when someone replies. AI handles the parts that used to need reading and writing: working out what an enquiry is about, tagging it, and drafting a reply in your tone.

People should stay in the loop for pricing and discounts, anything that sounds like a commitment, replies to complaints, sensitive cases and the first real conversation with a high-value lead. A reasonable rule is that AI can prepare and draft, and a person approves anything hard to undo. If the AI is unsure what an enquiry is, it should send it to a general queue, not guess.

How to set it up in four weeks

  1. Week 1: map the leads. List every place an enquiry can arrive, who currently answers it and how long it takes. Pick one source, usually the website form or the biggest ad campaign.
  2. Week 2: write the sequence. Write the acknowledgement, the first reply for your two or three most common enquiry types and two reminders. Decide the stages (new, contacted, quoted, won, lost) and who owns each one; our guide to setting up a simple CRM for a small sales team shows how.
  3. Week 3: run it with people checking. Let the workflow capture, tag and draft. A person approves every message. Note every wrong tag and awkward draft and fix the rules.
  4. Week 4: measure and widen. Compare the share of leads answered and the time to first reply with week one. If the numbers hold, add the next source or remove checks on the safest messages.

Questions people ask

How do I automate lead follow-up?

Put every enquiry into one place, send an instant acknowledgement, record the lead with an owner and a stage, and schedule the next follow-ups from a short written sequence. AI helps by reading each enquiry, tagging it and drafting replies for a person to approve. Start with one lead source and one sequence, then add more.

How fast should I reply to a new lead?

As fast as you can acknowledge it. Many studies of lead response time agree that earlier is better, but the exact figures vary by industry and most come from software vendors, so use them as direction rather than a promise. An instant automated acknowledgement that sets a time for a real reply is a safe default.

Can I send WhatsApp messages to leads automatically?

Yes, through the WhatsApp Business Platform, within Meta's rules. A person who messages you opens a 24-hour window for free-form replies. Outside it, you can only send pre-approved template messages, and marketing messages need the person's opt-in. Check Meta's current price list for India before you budget, because rates have changed.

Do I need consent before following up with a lead by SMS or call in India?

For commercial SMS and calls, India's telecom rules (TCCCPR 2018) require businesses to register senders and message templates on the DLT platform and to respect customers' Do Not Disturb preferences and consent. A lead who fills in your form is generally reasonable to reply to about that enquiry, but promotional follow-up needs recorded consent. Ask a lawyer about your own case.

Will AI-written follow-ups sound robotic?

They can, if nobody edits them. Give the AI your own past replies as examples, keep messages short and specific to what the person asked, and keep a person approving drafts until the output has proved reliable.
