AI automation

What an AI automation agency does, what it costs in India, and how to pick one

By Pluto Theory · 17 November 2026 · 9 min read

Short answer: an AI automation agency finds repetitive work in your business, such as lead follow-ups, data entry from invoices or sorting customer messages, and builds workflows that do it, with AI handling the reading and writing steps. As of November 2026, prices published by Indian agencies put one simple workflow at about ₹15,000 to ₹50,000, connected workflows at about ₹50,000 to ₹3 lakh and a production AI agent at about ₹3 lakh to ₹10 lakh, plus monthly running costs. Pick the agency that asks about your process before your budget, puts the accounts in your name and tells you what happens when a workflow fails.

This guide is for founders, operations heads and marketing teams who keep hearing that "AI can do that" and want to know what hiring someone to build it involves. It covers what an agency builds, what it costs to build and to run, the questions to ask, and when you do not need an agency at all.

What an AI automation agency actually builds

Automation means connecting your tools so that work moves by itself. AI adds the steps that used to need a person to read or write: understanding an email, pulling the numbers out of a PDF, summarising a call, drafting a reply. Most agency work falls into a few types:

Type of workA hypothetical exampleWhere AI comes in
Lead capture and follow-upA clinic's website and Instagram enquiries land in one sheet, get a WhatsApp reply within a minute and a reminder for the front deskReading the enquiry and sorting it by treatment and urgency
Documents and data entryA distributor's supplier invoices are read, checked against purchase orders and entered into the accounting toolPulling fields out of PDFs and photos in different layouts
Customer messagesAn online store's emails are sorted into returns, delivery questions and complaints, with drafts for the common onesWorking out what the customer wants and drafting from your own policies
ReportsA weekly sales and ad-spend summary arrives in the founders' inbox every MondayWriting the plain-language summary of what changed
Chatbots and AI agentsA website or WhatsApp assistant that answers questions from your documents and books appointmentsThe whole conversation, with a hand-over to a person

The good first projects share three traits: they happen often, they follow clear rules, and a mistake can be caught before it costs much. A five-minute task done fifty times a week is a better start than a once-a-month task that needs judgement every time. Chatbots and agents are a bigger build than workflows; our AI agents and chatbots page explains the difference.

What AI automation costs in India in 2026

There is no standard price list for this work, and agencies define their packages differently. The ranges below come from two Indian cost guides that collected the published prices of several Indian agencies (one published in August 2026, one in October 2026) and from one Indian agency's published pricing page. We read all three in November 2026. They are sellers' asking prices, not an independent survey, and most exclude 18% GST.

At the very bottom of the market, some agencies publish starter packages of about ₹15,000 set-up plus ₹3,000 a month for small template-based workflows. The guides we read share one warning: a quote of about ₹15,000 for "complete AI automation" usually means a ready-made template with your name on it.

What moves the price most is the number of systems involved, how messy the input is (clean web forms are cheap, handwritten invoices are not), how much testing a mistake justifies, and whether the build includes documentation and training for your team.

The running costs people forget

An automation is not a one-time purchase. Once it is live, you pay for the tools it runs on, the AI it calls and someone to keep it healthy. Budget for these before you compare build quotes:

  • The automation platform. As of November 2026, n8n's own pricing page lists hosted plans from €20 a month on annual billing for 2,500 runs, and n8n can also be self-hosted; one Indian guide puts a self-hosted server at about ₹1,000 to ₹3,000 a month. Zapier's pricing page lists a free plan with 100 tasks a month and paid plans from $19.99 a month on annual billing for 750 tasks. Costs climb with volume.
  • AI model usage. Billed per request, and the model you choose matters enormously. One Indian guide worked out that summarising 10,000 calls a month would cost anywhere from about ₹430 to about ₹43,200 depending on the model. Ask the agency which model it plans to use and why.
  • Messaging. WhatsApp Business messages are charged by Meta per message, and business solution providers often add a margin. Check Meta's current rates for India before you plan a high-volume flow.
  • Upkeep. Tools change their connections, forms change their fields, and workflows break. Indian agencies publish upkeep retainers of about ₹20,000 to ₹1.5 lakh a month; one guide suggests budgeting 15 to 25% of the build cost a year for a small system you depend on.

A useful habit: ask every agency for a written estimate of monthly running costs at your current volume and at double it. If the numbers only work at today's volume, you will find out at the worst possible time.

Questions to ask before you hire

  1. What do you need to know about our process first? A good agency wants to see the task done by hand, how often it happens and what goes wrong. If the first call is mostly about which AI model they like, be careful.
  2. Which tool will you build on, and why that one for us? n8n, Make, Zapier and custom code each suit different volumes, budgets and data rules. "We only build in one tool" is a limitation, not a strategy.
  3. Whose accounts will it run on? The automation platform, AI keys and any server should be in your company's name, so you can keep running it if you part ways.
  4. What happens when a run fails? Listen for alerts, a log of every run, safe retries and a named person on your side who handles exceptions.
  5. Where does a person stay in the loop? Ask which decisions the system will never take on its own, such as refunds, payments or replies to complaints.
  6. Which of our data will which AI service see? You need this answer for your own customers and for India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. A good agency sends it in writing before anything goes live.
  7. Is there a spending limit? Agents can loop. Ask for caps on runs, AI spend and run time, and for alerts before a limit is hit.
  8. What is in scope, and what triggers extra charges? Get the list of workflows, integrations, test cases and revision rounds in writing.
  9. What does support look like after launch? Response time, what is included, and whether your team gets documentation good enough to make small fixes.
  10. Can we start with one workflow? A small, fixed-price first project is the cheapest way to see how an agency works before you commit to more.

Red flags

  • Guaranteed savings or payback dates. An honest agency works out the likely saving with your numbers and shows its assumptions. It does not promise.
  • "Fully autonomous" for everything. If nothing in the proposal goes to a person for approval, nobody has thought about what happens when the AI is wrong.
  • Accounts in the agency's name. That turns a project into a dependency. You should be able to log in to everything you pay for.
  • No mention of running costs. A build quote with no estimate for platform, AI and messaging costs is half a quote.
  • Demos only, nothing live. A slick demo on clean sample data says little. Ask to see something that has run in production for a few months, or at least a detailed walkthrough of how they test.
  • A long retainer before any delivery. Upkeep makes sense once something is live. Paying monthly for "AI strategy" before a single workflow ships rarely does.

Agency, freelancer or do it yourself?

If the task lives inside two common tools and someone on your team is comfortable with spreadsheets, try building it yourself first on a free or low-cost plan. You will learn what the task really involves, which makes any later brief much better.

A good freelancer suits one or two well-defined workflows on a single platform. An agency or studio makes more sense when several systems are involved, when the work touches customers or money, or when you want someone accountable for upkeep. As with any hire, judge on process and how well they understand your business, not on the size of the team or the number of tools on their website.

One more thing to weigh: automations that face customers, such as replies, WhatsApp messages and generated content, speak in your brand's voice. Questions people ask

How much does an AI automation agency cost in India?

Prices published by Indian agencies and collected by two Indian cost guides (August and October 2026) put one simple workflow at about ₹15,000 to ₹50,000, a workflow with an AI step at about ₹45,000 to ₹1.2 lakh, a connected set of workflows at about ₹50,000 to ₹3 lakh, and a production AI agent at about ₹3 lakh to ₹10 lakh. Monthly running and upkeep costs come on top.

How long does an AI automation project take?

Published timelines from Indian agencies in 2026 run from about one to three weeks for a single workflow, three to eight weeks for a connected set of workflows, two to ten weeks for a chatbot or AI agent depending on integrations, and three to six months for multi-location enterprise systems.

Should I use n8n, Make or Zapier, or custom code?

It depends on volume, how sensitive the data is and who will maintain it. Zapier is the easiest to start with, n8n can be self-hosted on your own server, and custom code suits high volumes or unusual systems. A good agency explains why it picked a tool for your case rather than using one tool for everything.

Do I own the automations an agency builds?

Only if the contract says so and the workflows run on accounts in your company's name. Ask for the automation platform, AI keys and any servers to be registered to your business, for written documentation, and for a clause that hands everything over if the engagement ends.
