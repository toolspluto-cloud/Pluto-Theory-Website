A simple CRM setup for a small sales team: stages, owners and follow-ups

By Pluto Theory · 23 November 2026 · 8 min read

Short answer: a simple CRM for a small sales team has one pipeline with five to seven stages, one named owner for every lead, and one rule: no lead sits without a dated next step. Put every enquiry into that one list, write down what each stage means, and review the stuck leads once a week. A free or low-cost CRM is enough to start; the setup matters far more than the tool.

This guide is for founders and sales leads in India with a team of two to ten people who currently track leads in a spreadsheet, a WhatsApp group and people's heads. It covers when to move to a CRM, how to design the stages, how to assign owners and follow-ups, what the common tools cost, and where AI helps. Examples are hypothetical and show how the setup works, not results from a client.

When a spreadsheet stops being enough

A spreadsheet works while one person handles every lead. It starts to fail when the team grows or lead sources multiply. Signs it is time to move:

  • Two people have contacted the same lead, or nobody has.
  • You cannot say how many leads are waiting on a quote without asking each person.
  • Follow-ups depend on someone remembering, and a salesperson's leaving takes their leads with them.
  • Leads arrive from the website, ad forms, calls, referrals and WhatsApp, and each lands in a different place.

If none of these apply yet, a tidy shared sheet with the same stages and owner column described below is a fine start, and it will move into a CRM cleanly later.

Design the pipeline before you open any software

A pipeline is the list of steps a lead passes through from first enquiry to won or lost. Guides for small teams generally recommend keeping it short: roughly five to seven stages. Too few stages hide where leads get stuck, and too many make people skip updating them. Base the stages on how you actually sell, not on a template.

For example, a hypothetical interior design studio might use:

StageWhat it meansMoves on when
NewEnquiry received, nobody has spoken to the person yetFirst reply or call is made
ContactedA real conversation has happenedThe need, budget band and timeline are noted
Site visit or demo bookedA date is in the calendarThe visit or demo takes place
Quote sentA written proposal is with the customerThe customer replies, or the follow-up date arrives
NegotiationPrice, scope or timing is being discussedThe customer agrees or declines
Won / LostClosed, with a reason recorded for lost dealsNot applicable

Write one sentence for each stage saying what has to be true before a lead can enter it. Without that, one salesperson's "contacted" is another's "new".

Give every lead an owner and a next step

Two fields do most of the work in a small CRM:

  • Owner. One person, never a team. If you route by rule, make the rule explicit, for example by city, product or round-robin. A lead with no owner is the most likely lead to be lost.
  • Next action and date. Every open lead has a task such as "send revised quote on Thursday". A lead with no dated next step is a lead the team has quietly dropped.

Add a small set of other fields and stop there: source, product or service of interest, a rough value, the reason for a lost deal, and the date of last contact. Teams tend to abandon a CRM that asks for fifteen fields on every lead.

A follow-up rhythm that fits a small team

Decide the rhythm once, then let the tool remind people. A reasonable starting point:

  1. New leads: acknowledged the same working day, with a call or message booked.
  2. Quote sent: a check-in two to three days later, and another about a week after that.
  3. Gone quiet: a final polite note, then move the lead to a nurture list or mark it lost with a reason.
  4. Weekly review: twenty minutes on Monday going through every lead that has not moved in seven days or has no next step.

You do not need to build a CRM; several are priced for small teams. Choose the tool your team will actually open: if people cannot call or message from it, or it does not connect to the lead sources you use, adoption drops.

Where AI helps in a CRM

A CRM only helps if it is kept current, and updating it is the part people skip. AI can take over the clerical side:

  • Create the record from a form, ad lead or email, and fill in source and the enquiry text.
  • Tag the enquiry by product, urgency and location using your rules, and suggest an owner.
  • Summarise a long thread or call note into two lines on the record.
  • Draft the next follow-up in your usual tone, ready for a person to review and send.
  • Flag leads with no next step or no movement in seven days.

People should still decide pricing, discounts and anything that commits the business, and approve messages until the drafts have proved reliable. Customer details are personal data under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, so keep a note of which tools and AI services can see them and use business accounts. This is not legal advice.

How to roll it out in two weeks

  1. Days 1-2: list the lead sources, the stages and the one-sentence definitions. Agree who owns what.
  2. Days 3-5: set up the tool with only the fields above. Import the live leads from the spreadsheet, not the dead ones.
  3. Week 2: run the Monday review from the CRM. If something is not in the CRM, it does not exist. Adjust stages that nobody uses.

Questions people ask

What is a simple CRM setup for a small sales team?

One pipeline with five to seven stages, one named owner per lead, and a dated next step on every open lead. Add only a few fields: source, interest, rough value, last contact and lost reason. Review leads with no movement every week.

How many pipeline stages should a small business have?

Guides for small teams generally suggest about five to seven. Fewer hides where leads get stuck; more makes people stop updating them. Write a one-sentence definition for each stage so everyone uses them the same way.

How much does a CRM cost for a small business in India?

As of November 2026, published and reseller prices are roughly free for small teams on some plans, and about ₹800 to ₹1,400 per user per month for common paid tiers, billed annually. Prices differ between sources, so check each vendor's India page.

Do I need a CRM or is a spreadsheet enough?

A shared spreadsheet is enough while one person handles all leads. Move to a CRM when leads are duplicated or dropped, follow-ups depend on memory, or enquiries arrive from several sources.

Can AI update my CRM automatically?

AI can create records from forms and emails, tag enquiries, summarise notes, draft follow-ups and flag stale leads. A person should approve anything that commits the business, such as prices or discounts.
