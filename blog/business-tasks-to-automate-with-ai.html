AI automation

15 business tasks you can automate with AI this month (with examples by department)

By Pluto Theory · 19 November 2026 · 9 min read

Short answer: the business tasks worth automating with AI first are the ones that happen many times a week, follow clear rules and involve reading or writing text: replying to new enquiries, entering invoice data, sorting customer messages, chasing payments, writing weekly reports and answering staff questions from your own documents. Start with one task, keep a person approving anything that touches money or upset customers, and measure the time saved before you add a second. Below are 15 tasks, sorted by department, that a small or mid-sized business in India can realistically automate within a month.

This list is for founders, operations heads and marketing teams who want concrete starting points rather than a vision statement. For each task you get what the automation does, where AI comes in, and what to watch. Every example is hypothetical and meant to show how the work is set up, not to promise a result.

How to tell if a task is worth automating

Most businesses already use AI somewhere. Few use it well. McKinsey's State of AI survey, published in November 2025, found that 88% of organisations use AI in at least one business function, but only 23% were scaling AI agents anywhere and 39% could point to any effect on profit (figures as reported by IT Brief, read in November 2026). The OECD's survey of small and medium businesses, published in April 2026, found that 61% of the firms it surveyed use at least one AI tool, yet about three in four of those are beginners using simple tools for isolated tasks (as summarised by Entnerd, read in November 2026). That survey covered OECD countries, not India, but the pattern will be familiar: people use a chatbot here and there, while the real repetitive work still moves by hand.

The way out is to pick tasks, not tools. A task is a good candidate when it scores well on these five questions:

  • Frequency. Does it happen daily or weekly? A ten-minute task done thirty times a week is worth more than a two-hour task done once a quarter.
  • Clear rules. Could you write down how a good employee does it on one page?
  • Digital inputs. Does the information already arrive as emails, forms, PDFs, chats or spreadsheet rows?
  • Cheap mistakes. If the AI gets one wrong, will someone catch it before it costs real money or a customer?
  • Easy to measure. Can you count the time spent, the response time or the errors today, so you can compare later?

Plain automation moves data between tools. AI adds the steps that used to need a person: understanding a message, pulling fields out of a document, summarising, drafting. Most of the 15 tasks below combine the two.

Sales and lead handling

  1. Replying to new enquiries within a minute. Enquiries from the website form, Instagram, Google Business Profile and ad lead forms land in one place, get an instant acknowledgement on email or WhatsApp, and a person is alerted. AI reads the enquiry and tags it by product, budget and urgency. For example, an interior design studio could route kitchen enquiries to one designer and full-home projects to another. Watch: the first reply should promise a human follow-up, not pretend to be one.
  2. Qualifying leads and filling the CRM. Instead of a salesperson copying details into a CRM or sheet, the workflow creates the record, adds the source, asks two or three screening questions by message, and scores the lead on rules you set. Watch: keep the scoring rules simple and visible, so your team trusts them.
  3. Turning sales calls into notes and follow-ups. A recorded call or meeting is transcribed, summarised into decisions and next steps, saved against the lead, and a follow-up email is drafted for the salesperson to check and send. Watch: tell the other person the call is recorded, and keep drafts as drafts.

Customer support

  1. Sorting and routing customer messages. Emails and chats are read and tagged as order status, return, complaint, billing or other, then sent to the right person or queue. An online store could have angry messages jump the queue automatically. Watch: anything the AI is unsure about goes to a general queue, not the wrong one.
  2. Drafting replies from your own policies. For the questions you answer every day, the AI drafts a reply using your return policy, delivery terms and FAQs, and an agent approves or edits it. Over time, the safest categories can be sent without approval. Watch: the AI should only answer from your documents, and say so when it does not know.
  3. Sending order, booking and reminder updates on WhatsApp. Confirmations, delivery updates, appointment reminders and feedback requests go out automatically when something changes in your system. A clinic could remind patients the evening before an appointment, with a reply button to reschedule. Watch the cost: Meta charges per delivered message for business-initiated WhatsApp messages, and its developer documentation says that from 1 October 2026 each business number gets 1,000 free customer-service messages a month, with charges beyond that (checked in November 2026). Check Meta's current India rate card before planning a high-volume flow. Our page on AI agents and chatbots covers full WhatsApp assistants.

Finance and admin

  1. Entering supplier invoices and bills. Invoices arriving by email as PDFs or photos are read, and the supplier name, GSTIN, invoice number, date, line items and tax are pulled out, checked against the purchase order and entered into your accounting tool for approval. Watch: duplicate checks, and a person approving every payment. AI should prepare finance work, not move money.
  2. Chasing payments that are due. The workflow checks which invoices are due or overdue, sends polite reminders on a schedule you set, firms up the wording gently over time, and flags accounts that need a phone call. Watch: let the account manager pause reminders for sensitive clients.
  3. Checking expense claims and receipts. Employees forward a photo of a receipt; the AI reads the amount, date, vendor and category, checks it against your expense policy and flags anything unusual for the approver. Watch: the policy rules matter more than the AI, so write them down first.

Marketing

  1. Turning one piece of content into many drafts. A webinar, podcast, blog post or product launch note becomes drafts of social posts, a newsletter section and short captions, in your brand voice, for a marketer to edit. Watch: keep these as drafts. Output that does not sound like you costs more than it saves, which is why a clearly defined brand makes this task work better.
  2. Monitoring reviews and drafting responses. New Google and marketplace reviews are collected, sorted by sentiment and topic, and replies are drafted. Negative reviews alert the owner straight away. A restaurant group could see which branch keeps getting the same complaint. Watch: never auto-post replies to negative reviews.
  3. Writing the weekly performance report. Every Monday, numbers from ads, website analytics, sales and the CRM are pulled into one sheet, and the AI writes a short plain-language summary of what changed and what looks odd. Watch: do the maths in the spreadsheet and let the AI describe the results, because language models are better at explaining numbers than calculating them.

HR and operations

  1. Shortlisting job applications. Applications are collected from job portals and email, CVs are read against the must-have criteria you wrote, and a recruiter gets a ranked list with reasons. Watch: AI screening can repeat bias in your criteria or in past hiring. Keep the criteria job-related, review a sample of rejections, and keep a person deciding.
  2. Running onboarding checklists. A signed offer letter triggers the rest: accounts created, a laptop request raised, a welcome email sent, documents collected and the first-week schedule shared. AI answers the new joiner's routine questions from your HR policies. Watch: collect only the personal data you need, and store it in one place.
  3. Answering staff questions from your SOPs. An internal assistant answers "how do we process a refund?" or "what is the leave policy?" from your own documents, with a link to the source page. For a business with several branches, this is a quick way to help everyone follow the same process. Watch: it is only as good as your documents, so update them first.

What not to hand over yet

Some work should stay with people even when the technology can do a first pass. Payments and refunds, replies to legal or medical questions, anything that changes a customer's contract, final hiring decisions and responses to public complaints all deserve a human sign-off. A good rule: AI can prepare, sort and draft; a person approves anything that is hard to undo.

Data matters too. If a workflow sends customer or employee details to an AI service, write down which data goes where, and check it against India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and your own privacy policy. Use business accounts with AI providers rather than personal ones, and do not send more personal data than the task needs.

How to start this month

  1. Week 1: list and pick. Ask each team to list the tasks they repeat every week and roughly how long each takes. Score them on the five questions above and pick one.
  2. Week 2: write it down and build. Document how the task is done today, including the exceptions. Build the workflow on the tools you already use, with the AI step producing drafts or suggestions only.
  3. Week 3: run it alongside people. Let the automation run while the team still checks every output. Note every mistake and fix the instructions or rules.
  4. Week 4: measure and decide. Compare time spent, response times and errors with week one. If it works, remove the checks that are no longer needed and pick task two.

Questions people ask

What business tasks can be automated with AI?

Tasks that happen often, follow clear rules and involve reading or writing text are the best fit: replying to enquiries, qualifying leads, summarising sales calls, sorting customer messages, drafting support replies, sending WhatsApp updates, entering invoice data, chasing payments, checking expense receipts, repurposing content, monitoring reviews, writing weekly reports, shortlisting CVs, onboarding new staff and answering questions from internal SOPs.

Which task should a small business automate first?

Pick the task that happens most often, follows clear rules, arrives in digital form and where a mistake would be caught before it costs much. For many small businesses that is replying to and routing new enquiries, or entering supplier invoices. Measure the time it takes today so you can compare after a month.

Can AI fully replace people in these tasks?

Not safely, for most businesses. AI is good at preparing, sorting and drafting. A person should still approve payments and refunds, replies to complaints, hiring decisions and anything that changes a customer's contract. Many teams start with AI producing drafts only and remove checks once the output has proved reliable.

Do I need special software to automate tasks with AI?

Usually not. Most of these tasks can be built on the tools you already use, such as email, WhatsApp Business, Google Sheets, your CRM and your accounting software, connected through an automation platform like n8n, Make or Zapier with an AI model handling the reading and writing steps.
